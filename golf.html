Golf club events

A clubhouse is not a banquet hall.

Banquet centres are built for events. Clubhouses are built for golf, and the event room came later. That difference shows up in ways nobody mentions until the evening is already underway.

The rooms are usually smaller and harder — glass on one side, low ceilings, a lot of reflective surface. Volume that would be right in a hotel ballroom turns into noise here, and the fix is not turning it down, it is knowing where to place the speakers so the room carries evenly and people can still talk at the far tables. Dinner service tends to run on the kitchen's timing rather than yours, which means the gap between plates cleared and speeches starting is rarely where the timeline says it will be. And most clubs have a patio, which means half your guests are outside at the exact moment you want them inside for the first dance.

None of that is a problem if the person running the night has stood in the room before. It is a problem when they haven't.

What that means on the night

  • Sound placed for the room. Clean at the dance floor, conversational at the back tables, and not bleeding onto the patio or the neighbouring green.
  • A timeline that survives the kitchen. Built with slack in the right places, so a late main course doesn't cost you the dance floor.
  • Guests brought back in. Getting a patio crowd indoors for the moments that matter is a hosting job, not a volume job.
  • One person holding it. I plan it, I host it, I play it. No handoff at four in the afternoon to someone you have never met.

Three kinds of night

What I get asked to do at clubs.

Different rooms, different jobs, same person running it.

Weddings
The full day, or the reception on its own. Ceremony sound on a separate system so nothing gets torn down mid-afternoon, then hosting and DJing straight through dinner, speeches, first dance and last call. Most of my work, and the reason the planning starts months out rather than weeks.
Corporate tournaments
Shotgun start in the morning, awards and dinner in the evening. Announcements that people can actually hear, sponsor mentions handled without turning the night into an ad break, and prize presentations that move rather than drag. Sound on the course too, if you want it at the first tee.
Member celebrations
Milestone birthdays, anniversaries, retirements, club nights. Smaller rooms, older crowds, and a music read that has to work for three generations at once. This is where reading a room beats a playlist, every time.
