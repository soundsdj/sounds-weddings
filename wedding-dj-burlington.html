Weddings in Burlington

I am not driving in for your wedding. I already live here.

Burlington is where I am based and where I have spent most of my career. My office is on Upper Middle Road, which means almost every venue in this city is fifteen minutes from my door. That sounds like a small detail until you compare it to a company dispatching someone from Toronto who has never walked your room, does not know where the load-in door is and meets you for the first time at four in the afternoon.

Here is what actually matters about hiring locally. I can visit your venue before the day without it becoming a production. I can be set up, sound checked and out of sight long before your first guest arrives. And if your ceremony is at a church on New Street and your reception is downtown on the waterfront, I am not guessing at the drive between them.

The bigger promise is the one that has nothing to do with geography. Your wedding is a run of moments, and the moments live or die in the transitions. The entrance. The toasts. The first dance. That quiet stretch after dinner before the floor fills. As your MC and your DJ, I am the single person holding all of those threads, which is why there is no guesswork on the night. One person has been carrying the plan since long before it.

What I cover in a Burlington wedding day

  • Ceremony sound. A separate system with wireless mics for your officiant and readers, so nothing gets torn down mid-day.
  • Cocktail and dinner. The hour most receptions go flat. I treat it as part of the show, not a gap to fill.
  • MC hosting. Introductions, speeches, cake, dances, all led by someone who knows your family names before the day.
  • Reception DJ. A floor that stays full to the last song, read live off your actual room.
  • Uplighting and planning portal. Room lighting matched to your colours, plus a shared timeline you can edit any time.

Most Burlington couples end up investing somewhere between $2,400 and $3,500, depending on how much of the day they want covered and what they add on. I would rather tell you that up front than make you fill in a form to find out.

The unglamorous part

Load-in, parking, curfews.

Nobody books a DJ for logistics. But the reason a night runs smoothly is almost always something nobody in the room ever noticed.

Drive time
Everything in Burlington is inside fifteen minutes of my office on Upper Middle Road. Downtown waterfront venues, Tyandaga, Aldershot, Alton, all of it. There is no travel fee inside the city and no risk of the 403 deciding my arrival time.
Load-in and setup
Heritage properties and park pavilions rarely have a loading dock. Paletta and LaSalle Park mean carrying gear across grass and up stairs, which is a two hour setup, not forty minutes. I know both of those walks by heart, so I plan for them and your photographer is never shooting around a hand truck.
Curfews and sound limits
Waterfront and city park venues generally have to finish earlier than hotel ballrooms, and some have a decibel cap written into the contract. Your venue sets that hard stop. I build the timeline backwards from it so the last hour is the best hour rather than a scramble.

Burlington questions

The things couples actually ask me.

Do you charge a travel fee for Burlington weddings?

No. Burlington is home. My office is on Upper Middle Road, which puts almost every Burlington venue inside a fifteen minute drive, so there is no travel charge anywhere in the city.

How much does a wedding DJ cost in Burlington?

Most Burlington couples invest somewhere between $2,400 and $3,500 with me. Where you land depends on how much of the day you want covered and whether you add uplighting or a separate ceremony setup. I will give you a real number on our first call rather than a package sheet.

Are you the DJ who actually shows up on the day?

Yes. I am the only person who takes the mic at my weddings. The person you meet at the consultation, build the timeline with and email at eleven at night is the person standing in your reception. There is no roster and no substitute.

Can you handle both the ceremony and the reception?

Yes, and at properties like Paletta Mansion or LaSalle Park where the ceremony, cocktail hour and dinner all happen in different spots, that is usually the smarter way to do it. I have run both ends of the day at each of them. I bring a separate ceremony system so nothing has to be torn down and rebuilt while your guests are watching.

What time does the music have to stop at Burlington venues?

It depends on the property. Waterfront and city-owned park venues generally finish earlier than hotel ballrooms, and some have a decibel limit written into the rental contract. Your venue sets the hard stop. I build the timeline backwards from it so the last hour is still the best hour instead of a rush.

How far ahead do Burlington dates get booked?

Peak Saturdays from June through October tend to go twelve to eighteen months out, and long weekends go first. Because I only take one wedding a day and there is only one of me, my calendar closes earlier than a company with several DJs. If you have a date in mind, ask early.
