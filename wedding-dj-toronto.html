Weddings in Toronto

In a city this big, the question is who actually shows up.

Toronto has more wedding DJs than anywhere else in the country, and most of what you are choosing between are booking companies rather than people. You pick a package, you sign, and the person who walks into your reception is whoever was free that Saturday. Often you meet them for the first time on the afternoon of your wedding. That is worth asking about before you sign anything, whoever you end up hiring.

I work the other way, and it is simpler than it sounds. I take one wedding a day. The person you meet at the consultation is the person who builds your timeline, answers your email at eleven at night and stands at the microphone. There is no roster behind me and no substitute, because there is only one of me.

The other half of it is preparation, and Toronto rooms are less forgiving than suburban ones. Loading docks are shared with the rest of the building. Freight elevators get booked by the hour. Curfews are written into the lease. Your room may well have had another event in it the night before. I plan for all of that and arrive early enough that none of it becomes your problem. By the time your photographer is shooting the room, I am finished and out of the way.

And the promise underneath all of it has nothing to do with the city. Your wedding is a run of moments, and the moments live or die in the transitions. The entrance. The toasts. The first dance. That quiet stretch after dinner before the floor fills. As your MC and your DJ, I am the one person holding all of those threads.

What I cover in a Toronto wedding day

  • Ceremony sound. A separate system with wireless mics for your officiant and readers, so nothing gets torn down mid-day.
  • Cocktail and dinner. The hour most receptions go flat. I treat it as part of the show, not a gap to fill.
  • MC hosting. Introductions, speeches, cake, dances, all led by someone who knows your family names before the day.
  • Reception DJ. A floor that stays full to the last song, read live off your actual room.
  • Uplighting and planning portal. Room lighting matched to your colours, plus a shared timeline you can edit any time.

Most Toronto couples end up investing somewhere between $2,900 and $3,500, and that covers the whole night: ceremony audio, cocktail hour, MC hosting, the reception and uplighting. Toronto sits a little above my home markets because the day here is a longer one. I would rather tell you that up front than make you fill in a form to find out.

The unglamorous part

Load-in, elevators, curfews.

Nobody books a DJ for logistics. In Toronto it is usually the difference between a night that runs and a night that runs late.

Arriving early
I do not cut it fine in this city. I would rather sit in your parking garage for an hour than gamble on the Gardiner, a closed lane on Lake Shore or a game letting out downtown. Set up, sound checked and out of sight before your first guest arrives is the whole job description.
Load-in and elevators
Downtown venues often share a loading dock with the rest of the building and a freight elevator you book by the hour. Some rooms had another event in them the night before, so access does not open when you would like it to. I confirm the dock, the elevator and the access window with your venue in advance instead of finding out on the day.
Curfews and sound limits
Most Toronto venues have a hard stop written into the lease, and with neighbours close by the decibel caps are real rather than theoretical. Your venue sets that stop. I build the timeline backwards from it so the last hour is still the best hour instead of a rush.

Toronto questions

The things couples actually ask me.

You are based in Burlington. Does that matter for a Toronto wedding?

Only in your favour, because it makes me plan the day around arriving early rather than arriving on time. I build a wide margin into every Toronto date and I confirm load-in access with your venue in advance. I would rather be parked outside an hour before I need to be than let traffic decide anything about your night.

How much does a wedding DJ cost in Toronto?

Most Toronto couples invest somewhere between $2,900 and $3,500 with me, and that covers the whole night: ceremony audio, cocktail hour, MC hosting, the reception and uplighting. Toronto sits a little above my home markets because the day is longer here, with the load-in and the margin I build into arrival. It is worth asking any Toronto quote what it actually includes, because ceremony sound, a separate cocktail room and uplighting are usually billed on top, and peak Saturdays carry a premium. I will give you a real number on our first call rather than a package sheet.

Is the person at the consultation the person at our wedding?

Yes. Always me. Toronto is full of booking companies where you choose a package and meet your actual DJ on the afternoon of your wedding. There is no roster behind me. The person who builds your timeline is the person standing at the microphone.

Do you MC as well, or do we need someone separate?

I do both, and that is the point. Introductions, speeches, cake, dances and the music are all held by one person who already knows your family names, so nothing gets handed between two people who met that afternoon.

What time does the music have to stop at Toronto venues?

Most Toronto venues have a hard stop written into the lease, and with residential neighbours nearby the decibel limits tend to be real rather than theoretical. Your venue sets that stop. I build the timeline backwards from it so the last hour is the best hour instead of a scramble.

How far ahead do Toronto dates get booked?

Peak Saturdays from June through October generally go twelve to eighteen months out, and long weekends go first. Because I only take one wedding a day and there is only one of me, my calendar closes earlier than a company with several DJs. If you have a date in mind, ask early.
